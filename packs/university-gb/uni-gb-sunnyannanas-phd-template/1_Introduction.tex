\chapter{Introduction}
\label{chap:Introduction}

This document is a \LaTeX{} PhD thesis template structured to reflect a typical doctoral thesis at the University of Portsmouth. It provides a recommended chapter layout, pre-configured front matter, and working examples of common elements such as figures, tables, acronyms, and Ti\textit{k}Z diagrams. The template is intended to be adapted to your own research topic and institutional requirements. The remainder of this introduction describes the structure of the template and the purpose of each component.


\section{Recommended Thesis Structure}
\label{sec:structure}

The template follows a conventional PhD thesis structure and is divided into front matter, main chapters, back matter, and an appendix. The main chapters are:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Chapter~\ref{chap:Introduction} --- Introduction:} Establishes the research context, motivation, scope, ethical considerations, and thesis structure.
    \item \textbf{Chapter~2 --- Literature Review:} Critically reviews existing work relevant to the research topic and identifies the gap this thesis addresses.
    \item \textbf{Chapter~3 --- Methodology:} Describes the research design, data sources, methods, and analytical approach.
    \item \textbf{Chapters~4--7 --- Main Chapters:} Present the core research contributions. These may cover experimental work, results, analysis, and discussion. The number of chapters here will depend on your research design.
    \item \textbf{Chapter~8 --- Conclusion:} Summarises the findings, contributions to knowledge, limitations, and directions for future work.
\end{itemize}


\section{Front Matter}
\label{sec:frontmatter}

The front matter precedes the main chapters and consists of the following components.

\textbf{Abstract.} A self-contained synopsis of the thesis covering the aims, scope, results, and conclusions. It should be approximately 300 words and must not contain abbreviations.

\textbf{Declaration.} A standard declaration that the work is your own and has not been submitted for any other award. The template includes a draft declaration with a signature line; replace the placeholder name, location, and date before submission.

\textbf{Acknowledgements.} An optional section to thank supervisors, collaborators, funding bodies, and others who supported the work.

\textbf{AI Acknowledgement Statement.} A mandatory section disclosing the use of artificial intelligence tools during thesis preparation. See Section~\ref{sec:ai} for full guidance.

\textbf{Publications.} A list of any publications arising from the thesis, if applicable.

\textbf{Definitions and Abbreviations.} All acronyms used in the thesis are defined in the Definitions chapter using the \texttt{acronym} package. This enables consistent usage throughout the document via commands such as \verb|\ac{}|, which expands the acronym on first use and uses the short form thereafter, and \verb|\acs{}|, which always uses the short form. In this template, \verb|\acs{}| is further enhanced with PDF tooltips via the \texttt{pdfcomment} package, so hovering over an abbreviation in the PDF displays its full expansion. To add a new acronym, add an \verb|\acro{}{}| entry in \texttt{0.7\_Definitions.tex}.

\textbf{List of Tables and List of Figures.} These are generated automatically from the \verb|\caption{}| commands in the document. They appear in the front matter after the table of contents. Note that figures and tables inside the Appendix are excluded from these lists by design --- see Section~\ref{sec:appendix}.

\section{AI Acknowledgement Statement}
\label{sec:ai}

An AI Acknowledgement Statement is mandatory and must be included in all submitted theses. The statement should disclose clearly which AI tools were used, in what capacity, and at which stage of the work. If no AI tools were used, the statement should say so explicitly.

For students whose research involves AI as a subject or tool, the statement may signpost relevant sections of the thesis rather than duplicating material already covered in the body. A draft statement is provided in \texttt{0.5\_AI\_Acknowledgments.tex} with placeholder sections for writing and coding use.

The key principles governing AI use are as follows. Your submitted work must be genuinely your own: copying or editing AI-generated text, images, code, or other content without disclosure constitutes misconduct. All references must be accurate and verified by you --- examiners check them. AI tools such as grammar checkers may be used to suggest corrections to spelling, grammar, or structure, but you must make every change yourself. AI must not be used to reduce word count, as this risks losing your intended meaning. The viva voce examination may be used to test your understanding of the work where the examiner has concerns. Discuss the appropriate use of AI for your specific discipline and research design with your supervisors before use.

\section{Appendix}
\label{sec:appendix}

Supplementary material that is too detailed or lengthy to include in the main body of the thesis is placed in the Appendix (Chapter~\ref{chap:Appendix}). This includes, but is not limited to, ethics confirmation documents, UPR16 forms, extended data tables, and additional experimental results.

Importantly, figures and tables in the Appendix are configured to \emph{not} appear in the List of Figures or List of Tables in the front matter. This is achieved using \verb|\captionsetup[figure]{list=no}| and \verb|\captionsetup[table]{list=no}| at the start of \texttt{9\_Appendix.tex}. Any captions added within the appendix will still appear correctly beneath their respective figures and tables; they simply will not be listed in the front matter.

To demonstrate cross-referencing of supplementary material, the appendix contains three illustrative examples. Table~\ref{tab:example_appendix} shows a simple data table. Figure~\ref{fig:example_appendix} shows an example image. Figure~\ref{fig:tikz_appendix} shows an example Ti\textit{k}Z diagram. These can be referenced from anywhere in the main text using \verb|\ref{}| in the usual way.


\section{Thesis Structure Overview}
\label{chap:ThesisStructure}

The remainder of this thesis is organised as follows. Chapter~2 reviews the relevant literature and situates this work within the existing body of knowledge. Chapter~3 describes the research methodology. Chapters~4 through~7 present the main research contributions. Chapter~8 draws conclusions, reflects on the contributions made, and outlines directions for future work. Supporting material is provided in the Appendix.
