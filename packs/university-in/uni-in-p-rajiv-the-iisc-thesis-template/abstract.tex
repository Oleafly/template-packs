% Synthetic Chest X-ray (CXR) image generation has many uses, given there is scarcity
% of CXR data to train models. By leveraging generative adversarial networks (GANs)
% and diffusion models, previous works \cite{} have shown that synthetic datasets
% help mitigate this problem of scarcity. These synthetic images facilitate data
% augmentation for pathologies and improve model classification accuracy thus
% providing a scalable solution in this domain. In this work, we explore the
% current methodologies for generating high-fidelity medical images and evaluate
% their impact on classification accuracy.



Synthetic data generation has emerged as a promising approach to address data scarcity and 
privacy constraints in medical imaging. In this work, we studied potential of generative 
models for synthesizing chest X-ray (CXR) images, we evaluated Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) 
and diffusion-based models under a low-data regime. Our focus lies in generating disease-specific 
images. Through extensive experimentation, we observe that both GANs and diffusion models struggle to 
reliably capture disease-specific features when conditioned explicitly. To verify this limitation, 
we verified both existing cxr-image generation models and our own approaches viz. finetuning of diffusion models on MIMIC-cxr using 
1. disease labels 2. Complete doctor generated reports 3. LoRA-disease specific fine tuning etc., to understand underlying reason we 
verfied foundational models in CXR domain viz. Rad-DINO, Bio ViL-T. The main direction we verified is how well the generated images 
could be helpful in CXR disease classification. Through our experiments we found that synthetic image generation models 
struggle to generate images with specified conditioning.
